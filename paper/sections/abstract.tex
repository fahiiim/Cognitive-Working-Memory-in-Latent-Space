\begin{abstract}
Recurrent latent reasoners can solve harder problems by iterating longer, but only if they know when
to stop. We study termination detection in a setting where the answer is known. For local recurrent
models, a light-cone argument gives each input a lower bound on the step at which its answer can be
certified. The bound depends on the input's depth, not its length, and a local automaton attains it at
every length. We build DepthShift, a benchmark with exact per-instance depth and splits that vary depth
and length independently, and evaluate fifteen stopping rules on one backbone family. Jointly trained
pooled gates (ACT, PonderNet, TRM-style heads) leak length: ACT is 99.8\% accurate on deeper addition
inputs using fewer steps than the bound, through a global shortcut, and 35.9\% on inputs that are also
longer. Global convergence rules halt before the bound on most deep inputs yet stay correct, so
premature stopping and premature error must be measured separately. On addition, per-position
confidence halts at exactly the bound on every split, and the local completion heads we introduce match
it without improving on it. On segmented parity, the completion heads learn the training clock and
halt at the training depth; on inputs that are both deeper and longer, only rules that spend nearly the
whole step budget stay accurate. Termination detection beyond the training depth is
limited less by the readout than by training data that never shows an unfinished computation at a late
step. Code, frozen splits and every run are released.
\end{abstract}
