\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Scale.} Every experiment runs on one consumer GPU (RTX 5070, 12\,GB). The preregistered grid
(five seeds per cell, full maze grid) was cut to fit a five-hour training budget: two seeds per
method, three single-seed ablations, and a smaller gate-tuning budget than pre-registered for PonderNet
and the v1 gate (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md}, deviations). Our confidence intervals are bootstrapped over
test instances within each run and therefore understate seed variance; several comparisons that we
report as ties may separate with more seeds.

\paragraph{Mazes.} We could not train a maze backbone that passes Gate G1 within the budget (3\% exact
match on training-size mazes when stopped), so we make no stopping-rule claims on mazes. Whether local
completion heads help when information travels along corridors remains open.

\paragraph{Tasks.} The exact theory covers one-dimensional tasks whose determining radius has a closed
form. Addition and segmented parity are deliberately simple so that $\mathrm{LB}(x)$ is known for every
instance; they are not representative of natural-language reasoning. Our conclusions are about recurrent models with a known,
local growth rate; they do not transfer directly to models with global attention, whose light cone
covers the whole input after one step.

\paragraph{Backbone dependence.} A stopping rule can only be as good as the trajectory it reads. On
segmented parity the backbone fails Gate G1 (its stable-oracle accuracy out of distribution is below the
preregistered 95\%), so parity results measure stopping on an imperfect backbone. The progressive loss
was seed-dependent on addition. Termination detection and
depth extrapolation are entangled: a rule that looks premature may be reacting to a backbone that
never resolves.

\paragraph{What the theory does not say.} \Cref{thm:lb} rules out \emph{certified} early answers, not
correct ones. A rule that halts before $\mathrm{LB}(x)$ can be right on the data distribution, and on
addition global convergence detectors often are, because a carry wave keeps the hidden state moving
until it lands. We therefore report premature halting and premature \emph{errors} separately, and we
do not claim that premature halting is always harmful.

\paragraph{Selection and thresholds.} Thresholds are tuned on ID-val, with the cheapest setting within
0.5 points of the best. A different tolerance trades accuracy for steps differently, and gate
hyperparameters (ACT $\tau$, PonderNet $\lambda_p$, the v1 gate $\lambda$) were tuned on a small grid with
one seed. Stronger tuning of the jointly trained gates could narrow some of the gaps we report.

\paragraph{Broader impact.} This is basic research on small synthetic tasks. Better termination
detection lowers inference compute; we see no direct path to harm beyond that of adaptive-compute
models in general.
