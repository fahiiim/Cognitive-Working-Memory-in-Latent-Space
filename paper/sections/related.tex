\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Adaptive computation.} ACT \citep{graves2016act} and PonderNet \citep{banino2021pondernet}
learn a halting distribution that weights the task loss; Universal Transformers apply ACT per token
\citep{dehghani2019universal}, and SACT halts spatially \citep{figurnov2017sact}. These gates are trained
on the depths seen in training. We evaluate both as baselines on deeper-than-training inputs and
separate their sensitivity to length from their sensitivity to depth.

\paragraph{Recurrent models that extrapolate.} Neural GPUs \citep{kaiser2016neuralgpu} learn
long addition, but fail on long carry chains that random inputs rarely contain
\citep{price2016extensions}; DepthShift turns this observation into a controlled split. Recurrent
networks extrapolate to harder instances by running more iterations
\citep{schwarzschild2021thinking,schwarzschild2021datasets,bansal2022endtoend}, with the iteration
count chosen by the experimenter; path independence makes convergence a usable signal
\citep{anil2022path}, and test-time training can choose per-sample compute \citep{bao2025stop}. Looped
Transformers \citep{giannou2023looped,yang2024looped,saunshi2025latent} and the looped models of
\citet{fan2025looped} need ground-truth step counts during training and stop by oracle or maximum
confidence at test time. AdRNNs \citep{veerabadran2023adaptive} and ANIRA \citep{moosa2026anira} show
halting that scales to harder inputs, but harder means larger, so depth and length are confounded.
Implicit models \citep{bai2019deq} solve for a fixed point instead of choosing a step.

\paragraph{Latent reasoning and recursive models.} Recurrent-depth language models exit per token from
the KL between steps \citep{geiping2025scaling}; Ouro learns an exit gate \citep{zhu2025ouro}; Coconut
reasons in continuous latents \citep{hao2024coconut}. HRM \citep{wang2025hrm} and TRM
\citep{jolicoeur2025trm} train a pooled halting or correctness head, and their behaviour has been
analysed by \citet{ge2025hrm}. Several 2026 papers diagnose halting in such models:
stochastic stopping schedules and the correlation between loop count and length
\citep{kuo2026stochastic}, post-hoc confidence matching learned gates \citep{popescu2026halting},
latent motion dropping after the first exact solution \citep{krishna2026finish}, and states that encode
computational status but become less reliable at larger depth \citep{liang2026horizon}. We add a
per-instance depth with a provable lower bound, splits that separate depth from length, and a
local completion target.

\paragraph{Early exit and knowing when an answer is final.} Early-exit networks halt on confidence or
patience \citep{teerapittayanon2016branchynet,elbayad2020depth,xin2020deebert,zhou2020pabee}, with
calibrated guarantees in CALM \citep{schuster2022calm}; language models can predict their own
correctness \citep{kadavath2022know}. For reasoning models, early exit uses answer convergence or
dedicated predictors \citep{yang2025dynamic,liu2025convergence,nagle2026terminator,brown2026settle}. These
signals are pooled over the whole answer; ours is per position and is tested beyond the training
depth.

\paragraph{Length generalisation.} Arithmetic and algorithmic tasks are standard probes of length
generalisation \citep{nogueira2021arithmetic,anil2022length,deletang2023chomsky,zhou2024raspl,mcleish2024embeddings},
and shortcut solutions tie the depth a model needs to its architecture \citep{liu2023shortcuts}. We
separate depth from length explicitly.

\paragraph{Termination detection.} Deciding that a distributed computation has finished from local
information is a classic problem \citep{dijkstra1980termination}. Our completion heads learn a
termination detector for a neural computation, and \cref{prop:automaton} shows that a local,
size-invariant one exists for our tasks.
